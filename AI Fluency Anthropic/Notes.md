## What the Course Is About

This is the foundational course all the other role-specific AI Fluency courses (for Builders, Nonprofits, Educators, Students, etc.) build on top of. It introduces the **4D Framework** — a durable, tool-agnostic way of thinking about human-AI collaboration that stays useful even as specific AI tools and models change. The core idea: "fluency" is a different bar than "literacy." Literacy is knowing facts about AI; fluency is being able to actually *use it well* — effectively, efficiently, ethically, and safely.

## Key Concepts

### 1. Generative AI Fundamentals
- What generative AI actually is, at a working level (not deep technical detail, but enough to reason about it)
- Capabilities and limitations — where AI is strong, where it's unreliable, and why that matters for decision-making

### 2. The 4D Framework
Four interconnected competencies for working with AI:
- **Delegation** — deciding what to hand off to AI vs. keep human, and planning the work around that split
- **Description** — clearly directing AI on what you want and how (this is where effective prompting techniques live)
- **Discernment** — critically evaluating what AI produces before accepting it
- **Diligence** — using AI responsibly: transparency about its use, and accountability for the outcome

### 3. Three Modes of Working with AI
The course maps the 4 Ds across three different modes of collaboration:
- **Automation** — AI handles a task largely on its own
- **Augmentation** — AI assists while a human stays closely involved
- **Agency** — AI operates with more independence toward a goal

### 4. The Description-Discernment Loop
A key recurring pattern in the course: Description and Discernment aren't one-and-done steps — you describe what you want, evaluate what comes back, and refine your description based on what you saw. This loop is presented as the core mechanic of getting good results from AI collaboration.

## What I Learned

- "Fluency" is the right metaphor because it implies *practiced skill*, not just knowledge — you don't become fluent in a language by reading about grammar, and you don't become AI-fluent by reading about AI.
- The 4 Ds aren't a linear checklist — Description and Discernment loop back on each other, and Delegation/Diligence bookend the whole process (deciding whether to use AI, and being accountable once you did).
- The same framework holds up across very different contexts (creative, business, scientific, educational) because it's about the *collaboration process*, not any specific tool.
- This course is meant to be the shared foundation — the role-specific courses (Builders, Nonprofits, Educators, etc.) all assume you already have this mental model and just adapt it to a specific context.

## One-Line Takeaway
> AI Fluency isn't about knowing more about AI — it's the practiced skill of deciding what to delegate, describing it clearly, discerning the result, and staying diligent about how it's used.
