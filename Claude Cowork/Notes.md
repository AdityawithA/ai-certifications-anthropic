# Introduction to Claude Cowork — Notes

**Course:** Anthropic Academy — *Introduction to Claude Cowork*
**Track:** Product Training (non-technical, agentic desktop tool)
**Format:** Hands-on, self-paced — designed to get you productive in your first week
**Status:** ✅ Completed

## What the Course Is About

This course covers **Claude Cowork**, Anthropic's agentic desktop tool for knowledge work — distinct from Chat. The core distinction the course draws: Chat is a conversation, Cowork is a **working session**. You describe a task, Claude plans and executes it directly against your real files, folders, and apps — reading, editing, and producing real outputs on your machine — and you steer along the way rather than just exchanging messages back and forth.

## Key Concepts

### 1. What Cowork Is (vs. Chat)
- Cowork works directly with your files, folders, and connected apps rather than staying confined to a chat window
- The basic loop: you describe the task → Claude plans it → Claude executes it → you steer as needed
- Designed for non-technical users as well as technical ones — no coding background required

### 2. Your First End-to-End Task
- Getting from first launch to running a complete task: describing the goal, watching Claude form a plan, and letting it execute
- Learning how the task loop actually behaves in practice, not just in theory

### 3. Giving Claude Standing Context
- **Global instructions and projects** — setting context that persists rather than re-explaining it every session
- **Skills** — building repeatable procedures so Claude knows your vocabulary, your steps, and the outputs you expect
- **Plugins** — installing capabilities that match your specific work

### 4. Bringing Claude Into Your Tools
- Connecting Cowork to **Chrome, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and Outlook** — so it can act across the apps you actually work in, not just the filesystem
- File and research workflows that span multiple documents and sources

### 5. Steering Multi-Step Work Responsibly
- Practicing the kind of mid-task steering that keeps longer-running work on track rather than going off the rails
- Permissions — understanding what Claude can and can't do without your explicit approval
- Picking the right model for different jobs, balancing capability against cost/speed for the task at hand
- Habits that keep multi-step work **safe to share with your team** — not just safe for you individually

## What I Learned

- The Chat vs. Cowork distinction is really about mode: Chat is conversational back-and-forth, Cowork is delegating a real task and staying in a steering role rather than a co-writing role.
- Standing context (global instructions, projects, skills, plugins) is what turns Cowork from "explain everything every time" into something that actually saves time — the setup investment pays off on repeated work.
- Connecting Cowork into Chrome, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and Outlook was the most practically useful part — most real knowledge work is scattered across exactly those tools, and having one agent operate across all of them instead of copy-pasting between apps is the actual value proposition.
- Steering isn't optional for longer tasks — the course frames it as an active skill (knowing when and how to redirect mid-task), not something you only do if something goes wrong.
- "Safe to share with your team" was a good reminder that habits formed working solo don't automatically transfer — permissions and reviewable steps matter more once Cowork output affects other people.

## One-Line Takeaway
> Cowork turns Claude from a conversation partner into a working collaborator on real files and apps — the skill is setting up standing context once and steering multi-step work responsibly as it runs.
