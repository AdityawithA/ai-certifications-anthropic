## What the Course Is About

This course adapts Anthropic's core AI Fluency Framework specifically for students, applied across two key areas: **learning** and **career planning**. The starting premise draws a distinction between *using* AI and being *fluent* with it — most students have already tried AI for an essay or a problem set, but that's different from having durable, transferable skill in collaborating with it well. Rather than teaching prompt tricks or shortcuts, the course builds lasting skills through the 4D Framework, with a central emphasis on staying **"the human in the loop"** — maintaining agency, judgment, and responsibility rather than outsourcing thinking to AI.

## Key Concepts

### 1. The 4 Ds, Applied to Student Life
- **Delegation** — deciding what to hand to AI (first drafts, brainstorming, explaining a concept) versus what has to stay yours (the actual thinking, the decisions that build your own understanding)
- **Description** — clearly directing AI about what you're trying to learn or produce, so it acts as a genuine thinking partner rather than just generating an answer to copy
- **Discernment** — critically evaluating AI output in academic contexts — checking accuracy, catching AI's blind spots, and making sure using it hasn't hollowed out your own understanding of the material
- **Diligence** — being honest and accountable about how AI was used in your work, and taking responsibility for the final product either way

### 2. Two Core Contexts: Learning and Career
- **Academic work** — using AI to understand concepts more deeply (as a thinking partner, not an answer machine), while protecting the actual learning the coursework is meant to build
- **Career planning** — using AI to develop professional skills and prepare for a future where AI fluency itself is a baseline expectation, not a nice-to-have

### 3. Being "The Human in the Loop"
A central theme of the course: AI should enhance a student's own critical thinking and creativity, not replace it. The course frames this as a matter of *integrity as much as skill* — the risk of over-relying on AI isn't just weaker work, it's a weaker version of yourself as a learner. Staying in the loop means keeping ownership of your reasoning, your judgment calls, and your growth.

### 4. A Personal Commitment
The course includes writing a personal commitment to responsible AI use — translating the abstract framework into a concrete, individual standard for how the student will actually use AI in their own academic and career life going forward.

## What I Learned

- There's a real difference between AI making studying *faster* and AI making learning *deeper* — the course pushes toward the second, and that's a deliberate choice you have to make each time you delegate a task.
- "The human in the loop" is a useful test for whether AI use in academic work is healthy: am I still the one doing the thinking, or has AI quietly taken that over while I just review its output?
- The same 4D skills apply to career planning as to coursework — Description and Discernment matter just as much when using AI to prep for interviews or explore career paths as when using it for an assignment.
- Writing an explicit personal commitment made the framework concrete rather than abstract — it's one thing to agree with "use AI responsibly" in the abstract, another to write down what that actually means for how I'll work.

## One-Line Takeaway
> For students, AI fluency means using AI to deepen learning and sharpen career readiness — while staying the human in the loop so AI enhances your thinking instead of quietly replacing it.
