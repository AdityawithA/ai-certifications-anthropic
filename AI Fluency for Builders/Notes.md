## What the Course Is About

This course adapts the core AI Fluency Framework (the 4 Ds) specifically for **builders** — product people, engineers, and makers. The premise: builders are uniquely positioned to use AI to amplify what they create, but only if they approach it intentionally, with clear judgment and purpose, rather than just reaching for AI reflexively at every step. The course focuses on where speed of implementation and the human role in judgment create unique tensions for AI collaboration — a fast AI-generated answer isn't automatically the right one to ship.

## Key Concepts

### 1. The 4 Ds, Applied to Building
- **Delegation** — deciding which parts of the build process (scaffolding, boilerplate, first drafts of a feature) are appropriate to hand to AI, and which require direct human ownership
- **Description** — clearly directing AI with the right context (requirements, constraints, existing code/product conventions) so its output actually fits the problem
- **Discernment** — critically evaluating AI-generated code, designs, or content before it ships — catching subtle bugs, security issues, or misalignment with the actual problem
- **Diligence** — taking responsibility for the final shipped product regardless of how much AI contributed, and being transparent about AI's role where it matters

### 2. The Full Arc: Problem to Shipped Solution
Rather than treating AI use as isolated to one step (like "writing code"), the course frames builder AI fluency across the **entire arc** of building:
- Framing a customer problem
- Designing and planning an approach
- Implementing/building
- Shipping and following through
AI can assist at each stage, but the course emphasizes that the human still owns the arc — AI augments each step, it doesn't replace the builder's judgment about what's actually worth building.

### 3. Speed vs. Judgment
A recurring tension the course addresses: AI dramatically increases the *speed* of implementation, but speed without judgment can produce work that's fast but wrong — misaligned with the real problem, fragile, or subtly broken. Builder-specific AI fluency is about keeping human judgment in the loop even as AI accelerates execution.

## What I Learned

- Being a fast AI user and being a good builder aren't the same skill — the course pushes back on the idea that shipping faster with AI is automatically better if judgment gets skipped along the way.
- Delegation for builders isn't just "what task do I hand off" — it's about deciding which *decisions* stay human (problem framing, architecture, what "done" means) even when AI handles more of the execution.
- Discernment matters most exactly where it's most tempting to skip it — reviewing AI-generated code or designs *before* they ship, not after something breaks.
- The framework holds the same shape as the foundational course, but for builders the real risk isn't underusing AI — it's over-trusting AI-accelerated output because it arrived so quickly.

## One-Line Takeaway
> For builders, AI fluency means using AI to move faster through the arc from problem to shipped solution — without letting that speed replace the judgment that makes the solution actually right.
