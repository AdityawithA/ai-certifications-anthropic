**Track:** AI Fluency (role-specific)
**Developed with:** GivingTuesday, building on research with nonprofit professionals
**Format:** Short, self-paced, uses the AI Fluency Framework (adapted for nonprofit context)
**Status:** ✅ Completed

## What the Course Is About

This course takes Anthropic's general AI Fluency Framework and adapts it specifically for people working in the nonprofit sector — fundraising, communications, program delivery, operations, and leadership. The starting premise: nonprofits face constraints that most corporate AI training ignores — tight budgets, small teams doing the work of many, and accountability to multiple stakeholders (donors, beneficiaries, boards, communities) rather than just a bottom line. AI adoption in this context isn't just about efficiency — it has to stay aligned with mission and values.

## Key Concepts

### 1. The 4 Ds, Applied to Nonprofit Work
The course lightly re-covers the core AI Fluency Framework's four competencies, but through a nonprofit lens:
- **Delegation** — deciding which tasks (grant drafts, donor communications, data cleanup) are appropriate to hand to AI given limited staff capacity
- **Description** — clearly directing AI on tone, audience, and mission-alignment (e.g., donor-facing language vs. internal reporting)
- **Discernment** — evaluating AI output for accuracy and appropriateness before it reaches donors, beneficiaries, or the public
- **Diligence** — being transparent about AI use with stakeholders and staying accountable for the final product

### 2. Nonprofit-Specific Considerations
- **Resource constraints** — most nonprofits don't have dedicated tech staff, so AI adoption has to be practical and low-lift
- **Multiple accountabilities** — decisions affect donors, program participants, and mission outcomes simultaneously, not just internal efficiency
- **Mission alignment** — efficiency gains shouldn't come at the cost of the values or trust the organization is built on
- **Trust and transparency** — donors and communities may have concerns about AI use; being upfront about it matters

### 3. Practical Application Areas
- Fundraising and grant writing (drafting, not final submission)
- Donor engagement and communications
- Program evaluation and reporting
- Operations and administrative efficiency

## What I Learned

- The AI Fluency framework isn't one-size-fits-all — the same 4 Ds (Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence) look different depending on who you're accountable to.
- In nonprofit work, the biggest risk of AI misuse isn't inefficiency — it's a loss of trust with donors or the community if AI-generated content is inaccurate, generic, or undisclosed.
- Resource-constrained organizations benefit most from AI when it's targeted at *repetitive, low-stakes* work (first drafts, admin, data organization), freeing up human time for relationship-driven work AI can't do.
- Transparency about AI use isn't just an ethics checkbox — it's part of maintaining the trust that nonprofit work depends on.

## One-Line Takeaway
> For nonprofits, AI fluency means using AI to extend limited capacity without ever letting it dilute mission alignment or stakeholder trust.
