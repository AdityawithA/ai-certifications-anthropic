## What the Course Is About

This course adapts the core AI Fluency Framework specifically for pK-12 educators and school staff — built in partnership with Teach For America, drawing on their own AI fluency work with educators since 2022. The premise: educators are uniquely positioned to use AI for social good in the classroom, but the pK-12 context comes with its own constraints that generic AI training doesn't address — limited resources, accountability to multiple stakeholders (students, parents, administrators, the school itself), and work that's fundamentally mission-driven rather than efficiency-driven.

## Key Concepts

### 1. The 4 Ds, Applied to the Classroom
- **Delegation** — deciding which parts of teaching work (lesson planning drafts, differentiated materials, administrative tasks) are appropriate to hand to AI, versus what needs to stay the educator's own judgment
- **Description** — clearly directing AI with the right context (grade level, learning objectives, student needs) so its output actually fits the classroom, not a generic answer
- **Discernment** — critically evaluating AI-generated materials before they reach students — checking accuracy, age-appropriateness, and alignment with actual learning goals
- **Diligence** — using AI responsibly and transparently, being accountable for what ends up in front of students regardless of how much AI contributed

### 2. pK-12-Specific Considerations
- **Limited resources** — most educators don't have dedicated tech support, so AI use needs to be practical and low-lift within an already full workload
- **Multiple stakeholder accountabilities** — decisions affect students, parents, and school administration simultaneously, not just the teacher's own efficiency
- **Mission-driven work** — the goal is student learning outcomes, not just faster task completion; efficiency gains that don't serve learning aren't the point
- **Human-centered approach** — the course frames AI fluency as something that should support, not replace, the relational and pedagogical judgment that's central to teaching

### 3. Building on Existing Practice
The course explicitly builds on Teach For America's own AI fluency curriculum (in place since 2023) and their broader practice building AI fluency with educators since 2022 — so it's grounded in real classroom experience, not just the abstract framework.

## What I Learned

- The 4D framework holds the same shape across contexts, but for educators the stakes of Discernment are especially high — AI-generated content reaches students directly, so catching errors or inappropriate material before it's used matters more than in most other professional contexts.
- Resource constraints in schools mean AI's real value is in freeing up time for the parts of teaching that actually require a human — relationship-building, responsive instruction, judgment calls about individual students — not in replacing those things.
- "Mission-driven work" is a genuinely different frame than the builder or business courses — the metric isn't efficiency or speed, it's whether AI use actually serves student learning outcomes.
- Keeping the approach human-centered isn't just a value statement — it's a practical check: if a use of AI doesn't ultimately support students, timeboxing it against "does this serve the mission" is more useful than just asking "does this save time."

## One-Line Takeaway
> For pK-12 educators, AI fluency means using AI to extend limited time and capacity — while keeping every use accountable to actual student learning outcomes, not just efficiency.
